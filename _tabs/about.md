# Me

I'm Chris Miller, a software architect, Microsoft MVP, Pluralsight author, and longtime software developer living in the Albany, New York area.

I've been writing software professionally for more than 35 years. In that time, I've worked on just about everything from databases and desktop applications to web services, mobile apps, and embedded hardware. I've used more programming languages and development platforms than I care to count, although most of my recent work has been with C#, .NET, and cross-platform development.

I'm a Software Architect at Tyler Technologies, working on software for the K-12 student transportation market. That includes mobile applications, backend services, integrations, and the occasional piece of hardware.

I've always enjoyed projects where software has to interact with [something](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blinkenlights) in the physical world. Bluetooth, NFC, RFID readers, and other devices tend to make things interesting. Sometimes more interesting than I'd like.

## Microsoft MVP and Developer Community

I've been a [Microsoft Most Valuable Professional (MVP)](https://mvp.microsoft.com/) since 2013. One of the best parts of being an MVP has been getting to know developers from around the world. I've learned a lot from the people I've met, had opportunities to share what I've picked up over the years, and occasionally gotten an early look at what Microsoft has been working on.

I was also involved with the Tech Valley .NET User Group (TVUG) here in the Albany area, helping organize meetings and presentations for local developers. TVUG eventually petered out, but we had a good run.

I've been a judge and mentor for Microsoft's Imagine Cup and have spoken at developer events and user groups about mobile development, .NET, and related topics.

I also help moderate [r/dotnetMAUI](https://www.reddit.com/r/dotnetMAUI/). It's a place where developers ask questions, work through problems, and show off what they're building with .NET MAUI.

## Writing and Teaching

I'm a [Pluralsight author](https://www.pluralsight.com/authors/christopher-miller), with eight courses covering Xamarin.Forms and .NET MAUI.

My courses include:

- .NET MAUI Fundamentals
- .NET MAUI: The Big Picture
- Using Native Device Features in .NET MAUI Apps
- Xamarin.Forms Fundamentals and other Xamarin.Forms topics

I've also done technical reviews and written articles about cross-platform development and localization.

I like explaining how things work, especially when it's something I've had to figure out the hard way. Trying to explain a problem to someone else is a pretty good test of whether I actually understand it.

And if writing it down saves someone else a few hours of frustration, even better.

## About This Blog

I've been blogging since 2005. This is the sixth incarnation of the site, which explains the not particularly imaginative name, *Chris Miller's 6th Blog*.

Over the years, the blog has bounced between publishing platforms, survived hardware failures, and accumulated more than 600 posts. Some of those older posts have held up surprisingly well. Others are probably best left as historical artifacts.

You'll find articles about:

- C#, .NET, .NET MAUI, and Xamarin
- Android and iOS development
- Visual Studio and developer tools
- Windows, PowerShell, Linux, and automation
- Hardware integration, Bluetooth, NFC, and RFID
- Problems I've encountered and how I solved them
- Whatever else happens to catch my interest

A lot of the posts started with me trying to solve a problem while working on something else. Some turned into tutorials, others are observations, and quite a few are notes to my future self because I know I'll eventually forget how I fixed something.

I don't always write about the latest technology. Sometimes tracking down a weird problem in an old application is more interesting than whatever new framework was announced last week.

## Open Source and Stack Overflow

I've been active on [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.com/users/206/chris-miller) since it was in beta. I've answered plenty of questions there and learned quite a bit from other developers along the way.

I also maintain and contribute to open-source projects on [GitHub](https://github.com/anotherlab). Most of my work there involves .NET, mobile development, and small utilities that make development a little easier.

I've always figured that if I spent several hours tracking down some obscure bug, there's a good chance someone else will run into it eventually. I might as well write down the solution and save them the trouble.

## Outside of Software

When I'm not writing code, I enjoy photography, traveling, and tinkering with technology that has absolutely nothing to do with my day job.

Photography has been a hobby of mine for years. I particularly enjoy taking my Sony cameras along when we travel, although that usually means bringing more camera gear than I probably need.

I'm also the kind of person who will spend an unreasonable amount of time automating something that I could have done manually in five minutes.

Sometimes the automation even works.

And occasionally, those projects turn into blog posts.
